title:  Neurodiverse Support Needs in Human-AI Software Engineering Teams

description: |
 We aim to identify design guidelines for assistive programming environments that align with software developers' attitudes, behaviors, and desires toward the content, tone, and timing of technical, social, and emotional support from humans and agentic AI programming assistance. Programming is a technical, social, and emotional activity that affects learning, productivity, confidence, collaboration, and well-being for novice and professional software developers. Developers often need support for information seeking, unfamiliar codebases, debugging, code review, asking for help, communicating with teammates, planning work sessions, and managing frustration or uncertainty. Human support from mentors, peers, instructors, managers, and reviewers can be valuable, but it may also involve delays, social risk, unclear expectations, or concern about being judged. AI programming assistants may offer immediate support, but their usefulness depends on whether the support has appropriate content, tone, timing, privacy considerations, and escalation pathways to people. Understanding these preferences can inform assistive programming environments that support learning, productivity, collaboration, and wellbeing without replacing human support or pressuring users to accept unwanted assistance.
